Off-Grid Solar Battery FAQ

What is the difference between PWM and MPPT charge controllers?
PWM (Pulse Width Modulation) controllers act as basic electronic switches between the panels and battery, forcing the solar panels down to the battery's voltage and wasting up to 30% of potential solar energy. MPPT (Maximum Power Point Tracking) controllers dynamically convert excess voltage into higher charging amperage, providing up to 98% efficiency, especially in cold weather and cloudy conditions.
Why should I avoid discharging lead-acid AGM batteries below 50%?
Lead-acid batteries generate lead sulfate crystals during discharge. If discharged below 50%, the chemical sulfation becomes irreversible, coating the lead plates, permanently reducing capacity, and degrading the battery from 1,000 potential cycles down to fewer than 200. LiFePO4 batteries suffer no sulfation and can be routinely discharged to 85%–90% without harm.
Why is a Pure Sine Wave inverter necessary over a Modified Sine Wave?
A Pure Sine Wave inverter produces clean, smooth alternating current identical to grid power. Modified sine wave inverters produce a blocky, choppy square waveform that creates electromagnetic buzzing, overheats electric induction motors (refrigerators, blenders), and can permanently damage modern laptop chargers, Starlink terminals, and medical CPAP machines.
